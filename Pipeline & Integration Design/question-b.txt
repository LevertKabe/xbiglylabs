Question B: at 05:30 the pipeline fails halfway through processing, how do
I address technical recovery and communicate with stakeholders.

TECHNICAL RECOVERY
first thing I want to know is where it died, not just "job failed" but
which stage, ingestion, validation, transform, or publish, because that
changes what's actually broken and whether the failure took Gold with it
or stopped before that. this is basically the 05:30 SLA-at-risk alert
from question A firing, so the on-call page already tells me roughly
where things stand, since I'm timestamping every stage.

if it's a transient failure, cluster got preempted, a connection blip, a
source file locked mid-write, I let the retry-with-backoff do its job
first and just watch it, since checkpointing means it resumes from the
last committed stage instead of reprocessing the whole 20 million rows
from Bronze. I'm not going to manually intervene on something the
pipeline is already designed to self-heal in the two or three minutes
that costs me.

if it's not transient, bad data tripped contract validation, a partner
file half-written, an actual code or config issue, I pull it out of the
auto-retry loop right away, because retrying a deterministic failure
just burns the clock until 06:00 for nothing. at that point it's:
quarantine the offending file or partition so it doesn't block the rest
of the batch, and reprocess everything else that's clean. Bronze is
immutable and writes are idempotent MERGE, so re-running the clean
partitions is safe and doesn't risk double-counting.

then the real question is time, can the clean subset still make 06:00.
if yes, I keep pushing and treat the bad file as a same-day follow-up
outside the critical path. if no, I make that call by around 05:40, not
at 05:58, because stakeholders need runway to decide whether to run
loyalty processing on a partial dataset, delay it, or fall back to
yesterday's published table, and none of those decisions should be made
in the last two minutes.

STAKEHOLDER COMMUNICATION
I don't wait for a resolution before saying anything. the first message
goes out the moment SLA-at-risk fires, before I even know root cause:
"06:00 load is delayed, currently investigating, status update by
05:40." that goes to on-call plus whoever owns loyalty processing
downstream, over slack or paging, not email, this needs to land
somewhere someone's actually watching at 5am.

the second update is the real one, once I know scope: what broke, what's
affected (all of it vs one partner file), whether 06:00 is still
hittable, and what the fallback is if not. I give a number, not a vibe,
"publishing by 06:15, high confidence" is more useful to a downstream
team deciding whether to hold their job than "working on it."

if we do miss the SLA, I say so as soon as I know, I don't let
downstream find out because their job errored on a stale table. and I
give them the decision, not just the problem, run on yesterday's data,
wait for the delayed refresh, or skip today, that's their call to make,
my job is to hand them enough info to make it fast.

once it's actually resolved, I close the loop, what happened, how long
the real delay was, and whether reconciliation still checks out on the
reprocessed data, since "it's published" isn't the same as "it's
correct" after a mid-batch failure. and if this was something we could
have prevented, contract enforcement letting a malformed file through, a
checkpoint gap, that becomes a real follow-up item, not just a war-room
note everyone forgets by lunch.
