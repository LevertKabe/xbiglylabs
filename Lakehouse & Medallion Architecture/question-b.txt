Question B: what business logic belongs in Bronze, Silver, and Gold, and
what would I deliberately keep out of each?

rule of thumb: facts move left, interpretation moves right. whether a
record is valid or a duplicate is a fact about the record. whether a
customer counts as "valuable" or "active" is someone's interpretation of
that fact for a specific question, and interpretation belongs in Gold,
not baked into a shared layer everyone else has to inherit.

BRONZE
belongs: nothing business-related, just ingestion metadata (source
system, load timestamp, batch id).

keep out: filtering, dedup, joins, validation rules, identity resolution.
the moment you fix something in Bronze you've lost the ability to replay
it, and you've coupled raw ingestion to a business rule that will change
later. when it changes you want to reprocess, not re-pull from six
source systems.

SILVER
belongs: real types, dedup on a business key, identity resolution against
conformed dimensions (an S/4HANA customer, a CRM contact, and a loyalty
member resolving to one customer_id is a fact about the data model, not
a business opinion), referential integrity, SCD handling, quarantining
bad rows.

keep out: anything that encodes one team's definition of something, like
customer lifetime value or "active customer = purchased in 90 days."
different stakeholders define these differently, so baking one version
into shared Silver silently locks everyone downstream into whoever got
there first. same with one-off filters like "drop test stores" for
Finance, that's a Gold-mart filter, not a fact.

GOLD
belongs: the actual business logic. RFM/CLV, loyalty tier rules,
redemption rates, currency conversion, mart-specific filtering, feature
engineering for the DS/AI mart. this is where reporting, loyalty, and
marketing get to define things differently, on purpose.

keep out: quietly patching Silver's data-quality problems per-mart
instead of fixing the shared contract, and treating Gold as the first
line of defense for PII/regulated-data masking. pharmacy and the
external financial partner data need that enforced as policy from Silver
onward, not left to whoever builds the mart.

short version: Bronze doesn't get an opinion, Silver gets exactly one
opinion about what the data is, Gold gets as many opinions as there are
audiences about what it means.
